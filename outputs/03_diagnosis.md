# Alpenwerk AG: Organizational Findings

**For:** Internal strategy team
**Basis:** 12 survey responses (3 each from Sales, Engineering, Production, Finance) and the org map.

**Limitations:** The sample is about 10% of staff. Management, Quality and Maintenance are not represented. R06 gave only vague answers. Where the org map shows a project lead as "unknown", this may mean the question was not asked rather than that no lead exists.

---

## Strengths

**1. Dense cross-functional collaboration**
- *Evidence:* Every team names concrete partners in other teams. Sales works with Production, Engineering and Finance (R01, R02, R03). Engineering supports both Sales and Production (R04). Finance works with Sales and Production (R10, R11, R12).
- *Interpretation:* People know whom to contact, and informal networks work. This is a good base for formalizing processes.

**2. Cross-functional projects with named leads on the major initiatives**
- *Evidence:* Project Atlas is led by R03 and involves Sales and Finance (R01, R03, R10, R12). The ERP upgrade is led by R12 and involves Finance and Production (R07, R09, R10, R11, R12).
- *Interpretation:* Management can run projects across team boundaries, and the two most strategic initiatives have clear ownership. This applies only to those two projects, not to the smaller ones.

**3. Visible appetite for improvement and growth**
- *Evidence:* Respondents are working on a new pump series (R04, R05), export expansion and new distributors (R01, R03), CRM clean-up (R02), a lean workshop (R08) and supplier consolidation (R09).
- *Interpretation:* Employees are already working on many of their own pain points, so change initiatives are unlikely to meet cultural resistance. The risk is too many initiatives at once (see Weakness 3).

**4. Employees can name concrete problems**
- *Evidence:* 11 of 12 respondents gave specific, actionable answers to "what slows you down". R06 is the exception.
- *Interpretation:* The organization has a candid, problem-aware culture, and root causes can be addressed without a long diagnostic phase.

---

## Weaknesses

**1. Critical single-person dependencies**
- *Evidence:*
  - R01 waits days for technical input and says only Marco (R04) can answer most questions.
  - R04 says about half his week goes to quote questions instead of P7.
  - R05 waits for R04's decisions on P7.
  - R07 is the only person who maintains the capacity Excel, and R03 finds capacity data unreliable.
  - R12 holds three roles at once: Head of Finance, IT and ERP lead.
- *Interpretation:* R04 is a bottleneck for both revenue (quotes) and innovation (P7), so one person's workload slows two strategic goals. R07 and R12 are also key-person risks. If any of them leaves or is absent, delivery promises or the ERP project are at risk.

**2. Fragmented, untrusted data**
- *Evidence:*
  - Customer data lives in both the CRM and an Excel list that do not match (R02).
  - Customer master data differs between Sales and Finance (R11).
  - Product costs in the ERP are outdated (R10).
  - Capacity data is manual and unreliable (R07, R03).
- *Interpretation:* There is no single source of truth, and no one seems to own data quality. This slows pricing (the Atlas project), creates rework between Sales and Finance, and makes customer delivery promises uncertain.

**3. ERP upgrade is under-resourced and competes with other work**
- *Evidence:* R12 says the ERP upgrade has no dedicated people and that everyone does it on top of their job. R07, R09, R10 and R11 all list ERP work alongside their regular tasks. R10 and R12 also carry Project Atlas.
- *Interpretation:* The upgrade is the natural fix for the data problems in Weakness 2, so delays or a rushed rollout would affect the whole organization. The same few people (R10, R12) appear in several projects, which suggests overload.

**4. Weak handoffs and unclear ownership at team interfaces**
- *Evidence:*
  - Drawing changes reach the assembly line late (R08).
  - Several people order the same small parts separately (R09).
  - Sales cannot get reliable capacity data to promise delivery dates (R03).
  - Seven of nine projects have no known lead (org map).
  - R06 could not describe a clear role.
- *Interpretation:* Coordination depends on individuals rather than defined processes. R06's answers are a single data point. They may hint at unclear onboarding or role definition, but that is a hypothesis to check, not a finding.

---

## Recommendations (in priority order)

**1. Relieve the Engineering bottleneck**
*Addresses Weakness 1; based on R01, R04, R05.*
- Introduce a single intake channel for quote questions, with a response-time target and triage by deal size.
- Build a library of standard technical answers and options for recurring custom requests.
- Protect set P7 time for R04 and delegate routine design decisions to R05 or R06, ideally after a short check of R06's role and skills.
- Name an explicit P7 lead.

**2. Staff the ERP upgrade properly and use it to fix data ownership**
*Addresses Weaknesses 2 and 3; based on R02, R07, R10, R11, R12.*
- Assign a dedicated core team or backfill, and split R12's roles, for example by appointing a project manager or IT lead.
- Name owners for customer master data and product cost data.
- Retire the parallel CRM/Excel customer list.
- Move capacity planning into the ERP.
- As an interim step, document R07's Excel and train a backup.

**3. Formalize interface processes and project governance**
*Addresses Weakness 4; based on R03, R08, R09 and the org map.*
- Set a release routine and deadline for drawing changes to Production.
- Centralize or coordinate ordering of small parts under the supplier consolidation project.
- Agree a standard way for Production to give Sales capacity and delivery-date commitments.
- Confirm a lead for each active project and review the overall project load, especially for R10, R12 and R03.
